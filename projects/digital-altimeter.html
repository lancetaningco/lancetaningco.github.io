Digital Altimeter

Posted February 25 2026 | Category: Digital Altimeter

Overview

The digital altimeter is a compact device that measures altitude by monitoring barometric pressure. Built around an Arduino-compatible microcontroller and a BMP280 pressure sensor, it displays real‑time height readings on a small LCD screen. This page documents my thought process, hardware choices, and lessons learned while developing the project.

Motivation & Goals

I wanted to create a simple, self‑contained altimeter for hiking and education. Key objectives included:

  • Accurate altitude calculation using a barometric sensor
  • Low‑power operation on a rechargeable battery
  • Clean, readable display with minimal components

Hardware & Circuit

The heart of the system is the BMP280 atmospheric pressure sensor, which communicates with the microcontroller over I²C. I chose an Arduino Nano for its small form factor. The sensor and display are wired according to the schematic below.

Wiring notes:

  • VCC and GND from the Nano power the sensor and the 16×2 LCD module.
  • SDA/SCL lines shared between the sensor and a real‑time clock (future expansion).
  • A push‑button on digital pin 2 resets the altitude baseline.

Software

The sketch reads raw temperature and pressure values, applies the compensation algorithms from the sensor datasheet, and converts pressure to altitude using the standard barometric formula. A simple menu allows zeroing the altitude and toggling between meters and feet.

Results & Future Work

After a week of testing this device outdoors, readings remained within 5 m of a handheld GPS altimeter. Future improvements include adding Bluetooth telemetry and logging, as well as housing the electronics in a 3D‑printed enclosure.
